# Script hook and story policy

Start with the story, not an introduction. Before choosing the opening, silently draft at least six candidate first sentences using different approaches: curiosity, surprise, consequence, concrete specificity, an information gap, a question, or a contrarian angle. Select the strongest truthful option; do not show the alternatives.

Judge candidates by immediate comprehension, specificity, novelty, useful consequence, intellectual or emotional tension, brevity, and source support. Prefer a hook whose subject is clear at once. A bold or controversial framing is welcome only when the evidence warrants it; never inflate a finding into a cure, breakthrough, replacement, or certainty the source does not claim.

The first sentence creates a promise. The next line should quickly ground it in the most relevant fact, attribution, or qualification. Deliver the promised answer and its most interesting supporting detail; never use an empty tease or artificial “wait until the end” phrasing. Keep facts distinct from interpretation and label uncertainty naturally.

Build one coherent story: hook → reveal → explanation or escalation → implication/payoff. Each beat should answer a question while making the next useful detail feel relevant. Put the best information early when clarity allows. End on the implication, memorable conclusion, or a genuinely unresolved question that follows from the evidence. A loop is optional, never forced.

Write for speech: clear conversational words, mostly short sentences, active verbs, natural pauses, and only the jargon the viewer needs. Every sentence must create curiosity, reveal or explain something, advance the story, raise a supported stake, or deliver the payoff. Remove anything that does not earn its time. Use the shortest length that fully tells the story; do not pad to hit a quota or make every story the same duration.

Avoid greetings, “today we’re talking about,” “here’s what you need to know,” generic AI claims, repeated facts, unsupported drama, fake controversy, and conclusions that merely say the topic is interesting. Titles and descriptions must make the same accurate promise as the narration; no wording guarantees reach.
